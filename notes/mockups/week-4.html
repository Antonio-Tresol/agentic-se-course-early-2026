Week 4

From harness to factory

Track

Last week you ran one change through the loop by hand. This week you design the automated version for the same fork: which stages an agent runs, where a person checks, what an observer may change, and how you will measure the result against your Week 3 log.

Time budget

Pre-work: 146.7 min core, activity included.

Core: 101.7 min of talks and reading, then the 45 min activity. Optional: 93.5 min, plus one item not measured.

By the end of this week you can

0 of 4 ticked

Tick one once you can do it, after the self-check, the deliverable or the live session.

  1. Map the delivery loop for one repository into stages, each with a trigger, a committed artefact and a gate marked deterministic, agent or human, with a risk argument for each human gate.

    Rubric: Every stage has a trigger, an artefact and a justified gate

  2. Weigh a factory claim against counter-evidence, distinguishing output measures from outcome measures.

    Rubric: A critique with evidence

  3. Specify an observer loop by its signal, the one file it may change, and the check that keeps it away from CI and its own evaluator.

    Rubric: A bounded observer loop

  4. Choose the automation features for each stage within the subscription plan you hold.

    Rubric: The design fits the available tools

Pre-work activity

Guidance fades this week. Instead of a worked example you get the loop to design around: the stages your fork will run in the capstone.

On your own · 45 min, estimate

Factory design document

Write a factory design document of one to two pages for your lanorme fork, starting from the Week 3 log. It contains:

  • a stage table giving trigger, actor, committed artefact, gate type and risk argument for each stage;
  • the automation feature for each stage, chosen from the Claude Code column of the Week 5 table to fit your plan;
  • the automation feature for each stage, chosen from the Codex column of the Week 5 table to fit your plan;
  • an observer loop: what it reads, the one file it may change, and how CI enforces that limit;
  • a measurement plan defining a merged change and how human minutes, cost and failed runs are counted, plus one outcome measure;
  • one paragraph weighing Lloyd's thesis against one counter-source.

This document replaces v1's Harness Design Document. Its repository-knowledge and guardrail questions now sit inside the stage table, as the context each stage reads.

Deliverable: the factory design document. Bring it to the session for the design review. See the criteria.

Live session

Length
60 min

Bring your design document, and one piece of evidence from the core readings for the debate. We debate Lloyd against Horthy, review stage tables in threes, and test the designs against a real incident.

Five-minute retrieval opener

Closed-book questions on paper, for example: "Name the four parts Lloyd says a factory needs" and "Why does Horthy say a harness cannot fix maintainability?"

Plan for the hour

  1. 5 minRetrieval opener Active
  2. 15 minStructured debate. Half the room argues Lloyd's position and half Horthy's, each using one piece of evidence from the core readings: Lloyd and the playbook for one side, Horthy and Bender for the other. The facilitator closes with Bender's question of where the bottleneck moves. Interactive
  3. 25 minDesign review in threes. Each learner presents their stage table for five minutes; peers apply the first and third rubric rows. Interactive
  4. 10 minFailure walk. The facilitator presents Clinton's agent-to-agent incident; each group checks whether its design's identities and write limits would have stopped it. Constructive
  5. 5 minCapstone launch: success criterion, log format, fork rules Passive
