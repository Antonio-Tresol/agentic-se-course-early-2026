  1. Question 1 of 5 · choice

    According to Zach Lloyd in "Software Engineering Is Becoming Factory Engineering", what four essential components make up an effective software factory?

    Lloyd identifies four key components: automations to execute repetitive tasks, mechanisms to provide project context and skills, workflows that bring humans in at the right time when work is blocked, and self-improvement loops that refine skills over time.

    Source: Zach Lloyd, "Software Engineering Is Becoming Factory Engineering" (opens in a new tab), at about 7:55. Also Session 4 facilitator key.

  2. Question 2 of 5 · choice

    Why does Dex Horthy argue that adding more prompt guardrails or harness loops cannot fully solve the problem of declining software maintainability?

    Coding models are trained with reinforcement learning to make tests pass, and nothing in that setup penalises poor program design or eroded maintainability. Horthy says review agents and extra tokens can raise the floor, but the model is still constrained by what can be taught during training, so harness loops alone do not fix maintainability.

    Source: Dex Horthy, "Why Software Factories Fail" (opens in a new tab), at 11:21 to 12:57. Also Session 4 facilitator key.

  3. Question 3 of 5 · choice

    In "Software engineering at the tipping point", why does Adam Bender argue that testing and maintenance costs will grow far faster than code volume when code generation increases tenfold?

    Bender explains that dependency graphs between modules scale quadratically rather than linearly as code volume increases. Consequently, a tenfold increase in code volume can multiply the number of dependent tests and interactions by a hundredfold or more.

    Source: Adam Bender, "Software engineering at the tipping point" (opens in a new tab), at about 22:13. Also Session 4 reading check, Part 1, Q3.

  4. Question 4 of 5 · short answer

    Compare your answer with this one.

    Amplification magnifies whatever practices and processes a team already has, without steering their direction. Bender credits the idea to DORA: AI can produce more tests, documentation and code, but also more confusion. A team with good fundamentals can apply the amplification in useful directions, while a team without them gets more trouble.

    Source: Adam Bender, "Software engineering at the tipping point" (opens in a new tab), at about 32:36. Also Session 4 reading check, Part 1, Q4.

  5. Question 5 of 5 · short answer

    Compare your answer with this one.

    Horthy says to turn the lights back on and put code review back, but to plan up front so that review is easier: product review (the problem, the desired behaviour, mock-ups), system architecture (component contracts, data models, constraints) and program design, then vertical slices. He estimates that thirty minutes of pre-planning and alignment can save hours in review.

    Source: Dex Horthy, "Why Software Factories Fail" (opens in a new tab), timestamps 14:48 to 16:49. Also Session 4 facilitator key.
