  1. Question 1 of 5 · choice

    In the structured coding agent workflow described in ACE-FCA and "No Vibes Allowed", what do the three phases of RPI represent?

    RPI stands for research, plan, implement. Research is understanding the codebase, the files relevant to the issue and how information flows. Plan is outlining the exact steps and the files to edit, with precise testing and verification steps. Implement is stepping through the plan, phase by phase.

    Source: Getting AI to Work in Complex Codebases (ACE-FCA) (opens in a new tab), "What works even better: Frequent Intentional Compaction", which describes a "research, plan, implement" workflow. Also in No Vibes Allowed (opens in a new tab) at 7:41.

  2. Question 2 of 5 · choice

    According to the analysis in "From RPI to QRSPI", what key limitation in frontier LLMs caused single-prompt workflows with extensive instructions to fail silently?

    Frontier LLMs suffer from instruction budget overflow: after roughly 150 to 200 instructions in a single prompt, models degrade in consistency and silently ignore constraints without throwing explicit errors. QRSPI addresses this by splitting the workflow into distinct alignment stages rather than packing dozens of rules into one giant system prompt.

    Source: From RPI to QRSPI: Rebuilding the First Structured Workflow for Coding Agents (opens in a new tab), section "Instruction Budget Overflow".

  3. Question 3 of 5 · choice

    When starting an agent session at the root of a large repository or monorepo, how are per-directory instruction files (such as nested CLAUDE.md files) loaded into context?

    In a monorepo, loading every nested instruction file at launch would flood the context window with irrelevant guidance. Only the root instruction file loads at launch; nested subdirectory instruction files load on demand when the agent accesses or reads files within that specific sub-tree.

    Source: Set up Claude Code in a monorepo or large codebase (opens in a new tab), section "Layer CLAUDE.md by directory". Also in OpenAI Codex Docs, "Custom instructions with AGENTS.md" (opens in a new tab).

  4. Question 4 of 5 · short answer

    Compare your answer with this one.

    Errors made upstream are amplified downstream. A bad line of code is just a bad line of code, but a bad line of a plan could lead to hundreds of bad lines of code, and a bad line of research, a misunderstanding of how the codebase works, could lead to thousands. Reviewing research and plans therefore gives more leverage than reviewing code.

    Source: Getting AI to Work in Complex Codebases (ACE-FCA) (opens in a new tab), section "On Human Leverage". Also in No Vibes Allowed (opens in a new tab) at 17:11.

  5. Question 5 of 5 · short answer

    Compare your answer with this one.

    Goal evaluators judge only the evidence surfaced in the conversation transcript. A command like "unit tests pass" produces a deterministic pass/fail terminal output that the evaluator can verify directly, whereas subjective qualities like "clean architecture" have no measurable, concrete end state demonstrable in the agent's output.

    Source: Keep Claude working toward a goal (opens in a new tab), section "Write an effective condition". Also in OpenAI Codex Docs, "Long-running work" (opens in a new tab).
