Pairs

Number the pairs from 1. Pair p takes task ((p − 1) mod 6) + 1 in round 1 and task ((p + 2) mod 6) + 1 in round 2, so each pair meets two benchmarks and a class of 12 pairs gives every task four estimates. Learners enter their pair number on the Week 1 page, which marks their two tasks.

Plan for the hour

  1. 0 to 5Retrieval opener ActiveThree closed-book questions on this week's courses and the best-practices reading, answered on paper, then compared. The questions and key are in the deck.
  2. 5 to 10Debrief on the courses InteractiveWhat the learners tried in the courses: what worked, and what did not.
  3. 10 to 16Where the frontier stands, and the rules for the agent PassivePut the session's question: can you judge how much work an agent can complete on its own today? Show the top-five chart and the frontier-over-time chart. Give the three caveats in one breath: Verified is retired, Pro's public set has broken tasks, and METR's page is frozen. Show FrontierCode's example task for what maintainer-style grading looks like. Then each pair opens its round-1 card. The agent may read and run code but must not edit files, search the web, or open README.md, task.toml, solution/, tests/ or the linked pull request.
  4. 16 to 38Two rounds of scoping and estimating in pairs Interactive, then Constructive16:00 to 25:00, round 1 scoping: pairs run the setup command, give the agent the starter prompt, read its answer and probe it with follow-up questions. 25:00 to 27:00, round 1 submission: minutes, complexity, specification quality, the agent prediction and a one-line reason. 27:00 to 38:00, round 2 on the second card, the same way. Both estimates are saved before anyone reveals.
  5. 38 to 53Reveal and discussion Passive, then Interactive38:00, reveal: pairs reveal their two tasks; show every pair's estimate per task on a log axis against the ground truth, with the agents' results beside the pairs' predictions. 41:00, how far off were we? For each task, read the class median against the ground truth and the spread across pairs; on Verified tasks, count the pairs inside the annotators' bucket. 45:00, why? The pairs furthest from the ground truth on each side explain what they assumed; compare Tasks 5 and 6 on patch size against the pairs' complexity scores. 49:00, could we judge what an agent can do alone? METR's time-horizon chart measures an agent's reach in human minutes; test that task by task. Set the two Terminal-Bench tasks side by side, then Tasks 1 and 2, on human time, agent results and the pairs' predictions. Then set what the reveal says about Task 2's tests beside the pairs' specification scores.
  6. 53 to 60Exit question, then close ConstructiveLearners answer individually: “Could you judge how much work an agent can complete on its own? Compare your agent predictions with the published results. Which of your three numbers (time, complexity, specification quality) best predicted whether the agents solved your two tasks, and what does that suggest about how you will choose work for an agent this term?”

Modes follow the ICAP framework: passive, active, constructive, interactive. Grey notes are your moves.
