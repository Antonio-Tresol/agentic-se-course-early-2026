Week 3

One change through the whole loop

Track

This week you take one lanorme issue through the whole delivery loop by hand, with an interactive agent: triage, spec, implementation, review and a pull request. You time every stage as you go. Your log is the baseline that Week 4 designs against and the capstone measures against.

    Rubric: A complete, honest log

Pre-work activity

On your own · 60 min, estimate

Ship one lanorme issue by hand

Fork lanorme, enable Issues and Actions on the fork, and record the base commit's SHA. Copy one issue from a pool the facilitator curates into the fork's tracker. Take the issue through the loop by hand:

github.com/lanorme/lanorme (opens in a new tab)
  1. Triage the issue as easy or needs-spec, with a reason.
  2. For a needs-spec issue, write specs/<issue>.md stating the product behaviour and the shape of the code, using QRSPI's questions and research steps, and read it before implementing.
  3. Implement with /goal set to "uv run pytest tests/unit passes and uv run lanorme check . is clean".
  4. Review with a reviewer subagent or /code-review, and answer each finding.
  5. Open the pull request on the fork, where CI runs.

For each stage, log the human minutes as they happen, the tokens and estimated cost from /usage (Manage costs effectively (opens in a new tab)), the context given, every intervention, and any judgement a person had to make. The worked example log above shows one row per stage in this format. Enter the same values in "Your stage log" below.

Fork rules. Work on your fork, against the copied issue. Send at most one pull request upstream, and open it yourself. If you would rather use your own repository, it needs pull-request CI and at least three open labelled issues.

Deliverable: the pull request on your fork, the stage log and one paragraph. See the criteria.

Self-check

The self-check for this week is being written; the session opens with retrieval questions.

Live session

Length
60 min

Bring your stage log. We compare logs in pairs, pool everyone's minutes and cost by stage, and pick the stage to automate first.

Five-minute retrieval opener

Five closed-book questions, on paper. One applies a Week 2 reading instead of asking you to reread it: "Which of the article's long-horizon strategies did your log use, and at which stage?" The article is Effective context engineering for AI agents (opens in a new tab).

Plan for the hour

  1. 5 minRetrieval opener Active
  2. 10 minThe facilitator walks through the annotated example log Passive to active
  3. 20 minPairs compare logs. Each names their most expensive stage and the judgement it required; the partner asks one "why" and one "what if" Interactive
  4. 15 minEveryone adds per-stage minutes and cost to a shared table; the group discusses the spread and picks the stage to automate first Interactive
  5. 10 minExit ticket: the stage you would trust an agent to run unattended, and your reason Constructive

Tags give each block's mode of engagement in the ICAP framework: passive, active, constructive or interactive.

Deliverable and rubric

The pull request on the fork, the stage log, and one paragraph naming the stage you would automate first and the stage you would keep. Before the session, mark where you stand on each row and cite the log entry that shows it.
